Two of those fields have a disproportionate frontend impact. Branding has to be available before the first paint, otherwise the user sees a flash of the wrong colours before the app rebrands itself. On an app that's supposed to belong to the studio, that's visible and it breaks the illusion. And timezone has to travel with every piece of temporal data. I'll come back to this in detail, but let me plant it now: a class at six in the evening in Barcelona is at six in the evening for everyone, including a member checking from Paris.

Location lets a studio have several addresses, potentially in different time zones. Room carries capacity and the floor plan. Discipline carries a name and a colour, which feeds filtering and the visual coding of the schedule. And instructor matters more than people expect, because a lot of members choose the class taught by a specific person rather than yoga at six. So I'd plan for filtering by instructor, and for displaying a substitute when there is one.

Scheduling is where the modelling subtlety is, so let me spend a moment there.

A class template is the recurring definition. Vinyasa, every Tuesday at six, room two, forty-five minutes, this instructor, fifteen spots, one credit. It carries a recurrence rule.

A session is the concrete dated occurrence. That's what you actually book. It carries its start instant in UTC, its timezone, its capacity, its booking counter, its status, and the exceptions for that specific occurrence: a substitute instructor, a changed room, a cancelled class.

Which raises a trade-off: do we materialize the occurrences, or generate them on the fly from the rule?

Generating on the fly costs nothing in storage. But a generated occurrence has no stable identity, so you can't attach a booking, a cancellation or a substitute to it. And handling exceptions inside a recurrence rule gets unmanageable fast.

Materializing every occurrence gives each one an identity, and therefore the ability to hang data off it. But then, how far into the future do you generate?

The answer is a rolling horizon. We materialize a few months ahead, and a job regenerates over time. That reconciles stable identity with bounded storage.

And here's the real question, which I'd raise even if you didn't: what happens when the rule changes? The instructor changes for every Tuesday from September onwards. Do we modify the occurrences that are already materialized? All of them, or only the future ones? And what about the ones that already have bookings against them?

This is exactly the edit this event, all events, or all following events problem from Google Calendar. And it has a direct frontend consequence. The back-office interface has to ask the user that question rather than guessing, and the member app has to handle the case where the class you booked has changed instructor or room, with a clear notification.

Finally, seat exists only for classes with assigned spots. An identifier, a label like bike twelve, a position in the floor plan, and a type: standard, accessible, or out of service.

On the booking side, a booking carries the session, the member, optionally the seat, its status, and its idempotency key. Its lifecycle is a real state machine. Pending can become confirmed, or expire if a hold isn't confirmed in time. Confirmed can become checked in when the person shows up, or no-show when they don't, which may trigger a penalty depending on the policy. And either the member or the studio can cancel. Separately, a waitlisted entry becomes confirmed when a spot frees up.
