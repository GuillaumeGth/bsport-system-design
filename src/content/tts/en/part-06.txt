There's a point worth making here, because it illustrates the simple-first principle on a concrete case. The pending state only exists if there's a temporary hold while a payment goes through. If the booking is covered by a credit pack, the debit is instantaneous and we go straight to confirmed.

[medium pause] So I wouldn't introduce hold-and-confirm by default. It brings a time-to-live, an expiry job, a pending state, a countdown in the interface, and hold release on abandon. That's a lot of machinery, and a credit-pack booking doesn't need any of it. I'd add it exactly when a card payment sits between the intent and the confirmation.

The waitlist entry raises a product question I'd rather ask you than answer alone. When a spot frees up, is the first person on the list booked automatically, or do they get an invitation to accept within a time window?

Automatic maximizes occupancy, which is the studio's interest. Opt-in protects the member from being committed without wanting it, debited a credit, and possibly penalized for a no-show because they never read the notification. And the right answer depends on the delay: twenty-four hours before the class, opt-in is reasonable. Twenty minutes before, an acceptance window just leaves the spot empty.

[medium pause] That's a product call more than an engineering one. I'd probably go hybrid based on time to class, but I'd want to know whether studios penalize no-shows, because auto-booking someone who then gets penalized for not showing up is the worst of both worlds.

And on billing, the membership or pack carries the subscription type, remaining credits, validity, and restrictions on which disciplines or studios are allowed. That's what drives eligibility, and eligibility is a major point for the interface, because being able to book doesn't only depend on spots remaining, it also depends on the pack. I'll come back to that when I talk about interface states, because it's the point most people miss.

[long pause] Now, availability. This is a modelling point where it's easy to get it wrong, and getting it right is very visible.

[medium pause] Availability is not a stored attribute. It's a projection. Availability equals capacity, which is static, minus occupancy, which is dynamic.

A seat has no is-available field. It can't have one: bike twelve is taken at six and free at seven. Availability is a property of the pair, seat and session, not of the seat. If you store it on the seat, your model breaks at the second session of the day.

The consequence for the contract is that the client consumes a list of seat availability, seat identifier plus status, per session, kept separate from the floor plan.

There are two regimes. Anonymous capacity, where there are no individual spots, just a counter, so the session carries capacity and booked count. And assigned seats, where there are real seat entities and availability is computed seat by seat.

The point to make is that the second contains the first. If you have seats, you can derive the counter. But you don't make classes that don't need a floor plan pay for the complexity of one. The floor plan is optional, and the interface switches between a simple flow and the seat selector depending on whether one exists.
