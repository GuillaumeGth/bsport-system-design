There's a second, subtler point about these states: some of them expire on their own. At one minute to six, cancellation is free. At one minute past six, it isn't, and no server message told you. It's a rare case, data that becomes false purely through the passage of time. The consequence is that you need either a timer that recomputes at the deadline, or revalidation when the window regains focus, or, most importantly, a recheck at the moment of the action. Never authorize a free cancellation on the strength of a state rendered ten minutes ago.

And on the journey itself, I'd model it as an explicit state machine. Idle, then confirming, which shows the recap: class, instructor, time, cost. From there, selecting a seat if the class has assigned spots. Paying if it isn't covered by the pack. Submitting, which is where the optimistic update already shows the result. Then success, with confirmation and add-to-calendar. Or failed, with a typed reason: seat taken sends me back to the selector, session full offers the waitlist, payment failed offers another method, and a network failure retries automatically with the same key.

The trade-off there is state machine versus booleans. Five booleans, is-loading, is-error, is-success, has-seat, is-paying, give you thirty-two combinations, the vast majority of which are impossible but still representable. And anything representable is reachable by a bug. On top of that the rendering logic becomes a cascade of nested conditions nobody dares refactor.

A discriminated union makes impossible states unrepresentable, forces TypeScript to handle every case, and keeps state-specific data inside the state it belongs to. The failure reason only exists in the failed state, so you can't accidentally read it anywhere else.

A formal state machine library like XState adds transition validation and visualization, at the cost of a dependency and a learning curve.

My answer is a discriminated union in TypeScript, with no dependency. You get most of the guarantee for zero cost. XState if the graph genuinely becomes complex, with parallel states, nested states, history.
